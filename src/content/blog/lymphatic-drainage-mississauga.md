Lymphatic drainage is the most misunderstood treatment on our menu.

It's marketed everywhere as a detox and a body-contouring service, and that's buried what it's good at. Which is a shame, because there are two groups of people it helps a lot.

## What the lymphatic system does

Your circulatory system pushes blood around under pressure from the heart. The lymphatic system is the return route, for fluid, proteins and immune cells that leave the bloodstream and end up in your tissues.

Unlike blood, lymph has no pump. It moves through vessels and nodes, propelled by muscle contraction, breathing, and the vessel walls squeezing themselves.

Because there's no pump, lymph moves slowly and depends on movement. Overload the system — after surgery, after nodes are removed or damaged, during pregnancy, after being immobile — and fluid builds up in the tissue.

That's swelling. That's what this treatment is for.

## What the treatment feels like

Almost nothing like a normal massage.

It's very light, slow and rhythmic, pressure comparable to stretching the skin rather than working into muscle. We work in a specific sequence, clearing the areas closest to the drainage points first before moving outward, because pushing fluid toward a congested area achieves nothing.

If you booked expecting deep pressure, it'll feel underwhelming. That's correct and intentional. Heavy pressure collapses the superficial lymphatic vessels, which sit just under the skin, and works directly against the treatment.

## Who benefits

**1. Post-surgical swelling — the strongest case.**

After surgery, tissue swells. Where lymph nodes have been removed or vessels disrupted, that swelling can persist. This is the best-established use of manual lymphatic drainage and where clinical evidence is strongest, particularly as one part of the standard approach to lymphoedema, which also includes compression and exercise.

Most common reasons people come to us: post-operative swelling after orthopaedic surgery, after cosmetic or reconstructive procedures, and following breast surgery involving node removal.

Important: if you've had lymph nodes removed or have diagnosed lymphoedema, that's a specific clinical situation. It needs clearance from your surgeon or specialist and, ideally, a therapist with specific lymphoedema training. Tell us at booking so we can get you to the right person.

**2. Pregnancy and post-natal swelling.**

Fluid retention in the legs, ankles and feet is one of the most common physical complaints of later pregnancy — increased blood volume, hormonal changes, and a growing uterus reducing venous return all contribute. Lymphatic work is gentle, generally well tolerated, and a lot of people find it relieving.

Post-natally, especially after a caesarean, the same techniques help with swelling and comfort. It pairs naturally with prenatal massage, and we can position you comfortably at any stage.

One necessary caution. Swelling in pregnancy is usually normal. But sudden or severe swelling, especially in the face or hands, or with headache, visual changes or upper abdominal pain, can indicate pre-eclampsia and needs same-day medical assessment, not a massage appointment. And swelling in one leg only, with pain, warmth or redness, needs urgent assessment to rule out a blood clot.

**3. General heaviness and puffiness.**

Long flights, extended desk hours, standing all day, a spell in a cast or boot. The swelling is minor and would resolve anyway, but the treatment is pleasant and often relieving. Just be clear-eyed that this is comfort, not medicine.

## Where the claims outrun the evidence

Blunt version, because this field oversells badly.

**"Detoxification."** The lymphatic system doesn't remove toxins in the sense wellness marketing implies. That job is mostly your liver and kidneys. Lymphatic drainage does not detox you and nobody should tell you it does.

**Weight loss and body contouring.** Lymphatic drainage moves fluid. Fluid returns. Any change in your measurements is temporary fluid redistribution, not fat loss. So-called "Brazilian lymphatic drainage" is marketed hard on contouring claims — the technique may be pleasant and may briefly reduce fluid, but it doesn't reduce body fat, and no honest practitioner will tell you it does.

**Immune boosting.** The lymphatic system is central to immune function, but there's no good evidence that massaging it makes you more resistant to illness.

**Cellulite.** No.

What we can defend: for genuine fluid accumulation (post-surgical, lymphoedema-related, pregnancy-related) it is a legitimate and useful treatment with reasonable clinical support. Outside those situations it's a comfortable treatment with a modest, short-lived effect.

## Who shouldn't have it

This treatment increases fluid movement through the system, which isn't appropriate for everyone. Avoid, or get medical clearance first, if you have:

1. Acute infection including cellulitis, or an unexplained fever
2. Congestive heart failure or uncontrolled high blood pressure — increasing fluid return can overload a compromised heart
3. A known or suspected blood clot
4. Active cancer, without clearance from your oncology team
5. Acute kidney problems
6. Any undiagnosed swelling, particularly in one limb only

Unsure? Call before booking. This is the one treatment on our list where contraindications matter, and any clinic that doesn't ask you about them isn't paying attention.

## What actually supports healing, since we're being honest about detox

We spent a section above explaining that this treatment doesn't detoxify anyone. It seems only fair to say what genuinely does support tissue healing, because there is a real answer and it's less exciting than the marketing.

After surgery, the things that matter are adequate protein, enough overall energy, and micronutrient status — particularly vitamin C and zinc, both of which are involved in wound healing and collagen formation. Hydration matters. Iron matters if you lost blood.

None of that is our scope. Massage therapists are regulated for hands-on treatment, not nutrition.

It is squarely the scope of a naturopathic doctor. Naturopathic medicine is regulated in Ontario by the College of Naturopaths of Ontario, and NDs can draw blood for certain tests and prescribe from a defined list, which is exactly what you want for post-surgical recovery where guessing is unhelpful. Our naturopathic doctors are Dr. Jennifer Tang and Lisbeth Bitar Patino. Coordinate anything post-surgical with your surgeon as well.

The honest packaging: we can move fluid and make you more comfortable. Someone else should make sure your body has the raw materials to rebuild. Those are different jobs and they work well together.
