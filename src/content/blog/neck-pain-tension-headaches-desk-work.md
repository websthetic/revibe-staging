It usually starts as a stiff neck by mid-afternoon. A few weeks later there's a dull headache most days, sitting at the base of the skull or wrapping around to the forehead. By Friday you're turning your whole body to check your blind spot.

If that sounds familiar, you're far from alone. Neck pain from desk work, often with tension-type headaches, is one of the most common reasons people book with us, especially people who spend most of the day at a screen.

## Why desk work causes neck pain

The neck isn't damaged by sitting. The problem is that it does the same low-level work for hours with no break. Holding your head forward toward a screen keeps the muscles at the back of your neck and upper shoulders working constantly. Over time they get sore and stiff, and the joints at the top of the neck can become irritated.

Some headaches start in the neck itself. These are called cervicogenic headaches. Others are tension-type headaches, where tight, sensitive muscles in the neck and shoulders play a part. They often overlap, and both tend to get worse as the day goes on.

Stress, poor sleep and long stretches without moving all add to it. If sleep is part of the problem, our naturopathic doctors run a [sleep and energy program](/services/naturopathic-medicine/sleep-and-energy/).

## How long it takes

Most neck pain, including neck pain with tension headaches, improves over a 6 to 12 week plan before we review it. We split that into four stages:

| Stage | Typical weeks | What's happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restore | Weeks 0 to 2 | Ease the pain, free up movement, set up your desk |
| Rebuild | Weeks 1 to 6 | Deep neck and shoulder blade strength |
| Retrain | Weeks 4 to 8 | Full workdays, training and sleep without the neck flaring up |
| Perform | Weeks 6 to 12 and on | Keep it strong so it doesn't come back |

Pinched nerves, where pain or tingling runs into the arm, and neck pain after a car accident usually take longer. Your assessment sets the timeline for your neck. Lower back pain follows a similar path; see [how long low back pain takes to heal](/low-back-pain-recovery-time/).

## What actually helps

**Hands-on treatment.** [Chiropractic](/services/chiropractic/) adjustments, mobilization and soft tissue work such as [massage therapy](/services/massage/) can ease pain and stiffness, especially early on. It works best combined with exercise rather than on its own.

**Specific exercise.** This is the part that keeps the pain from coming back. We focus on the deep muscles at the front of the neck, the muscles between the shoulder blades, and getting the upper back moving again. These exercises are simple and take a few minutes a day.

**Movement through the day.** Getting up every 30 to 45 minutes does more than any chair. Stand up, roll your shoulders, look around the room, walk to get water.

**Sleep.** A pillow that keeps your neck level with your body, whether you sleep on your back or side, can make mornings easier.

## Desk changes that make a difference

You don't need an expensive setup. Start with these:

1. **Screen at eye level.** The top of the screen should sit roughly at eye height, about an arm's length away.
2. **Laptop on a stand.** Then use a separate keyboard and mouse so your arms stay relaxed.
3. **Chair height.** Feet flat, hips slightly higher than knees, and your back supported.
4. **Phone up, not down.** Bring your phone toward eye level instead of dropping your head to it.
5. **Change position.** Alternate between sitting and standing if you can. The best posture is the next one.

## When to see a doctor first

Go to emergency or see a doctor right away if you have:

- A sudden, severe headache, especially the worst you've ever had
- A headache with fever, a stiff neck and feeling unwell
- Neck pain or headache after a fall, accident or blow to the head
- Weakness, numbness or tingling in your arms that is getting worse
- Changes in vision, trouble speaking, or dizziness with the headache
