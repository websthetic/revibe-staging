Every January the gym fills up with people starting a new plan. By March most of them are gone. It's rarely about willpower. Most plans are copied from somewhere else, aren't built for the person doing them, and give no proof of progress. Without proof, motivation fades.

If you're looking at [performance training in Mississauga](/location/mississauga/performance-training/) or a [strength and conditioning coach in Markham](/location/markham/performance-training/), there's a better way to start the year: find out where you actually are before you decide what to do.

## Test first, then train

A baseline test gives you real numbers to build from. At Revibe it starts with five countermovement jumps on our force plates. Our post on [force plate testing](/force-plate-testing-explained/) explains each score in more detail. From those jumps we score how you brake, how much force you produce, how quickly you turn that force into a jump, and how much power you end up with. The plates read each foot, so we also see how evenly your two sides share the work.

Depending on your goal, we add timed sprints and strength testing.

You get a written baseline report that shows your strengths, the areas holding you back, and how you compare with a D1 and pro benchmark. Those priorities become your program.

## What a training block looks like

| Block | Best for | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 sessions | One focused goal, like a stronger squat or a faster first step | Baseline test, a focused block, retest |
| 24 sessions | Most people. Enough time to see real change | The full test, train and retest cycle |
| 48 sessions | A full season or year | Training in phases, planned around your calendar, with retests along the way |

Your coach writes the program and coaches every session in person. Most people come two to three times a week. At the end of the block we run the same tests again and you get a new report, so you can see exactly what changed.

## Performance training vs personal training

They can look similar from the outside. The difference is in how the program is built and checked.

| | Performance training at Revibe | Typical personal training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starts with | A baseline test on force plates | A conversation and maybe a few exercises |
| Program built from | Your numbers and your goal | The trainer's usual program |
| Progress measured by | A retest with the same tests | How the workouts feel |
| Injury support | Coaches work beside [physiotherapists](/services/physiotherapy/) | Usually a referral elsewhere |

If you just want company and accountability, a personal trainer can be a good fit. If you want to know your training is working, start with a test.
