Ask most coaches how their team is doing physically and you'll get an answer based on what they see in practice. Who looks quick. Who looks tired. Who never seems to get to loose balls.

That's useful, but it's a guess. Team combine testing replaces the guess with numbers for every player, measured the same way.

## What gets tested

A Revibe combine is built around four stations. Athletes rotate through in small groups.

**Force plate jumps.** Each player does a set of countermovement jumps on our [force plates](/force-plate-testing-explained/). The plates read each foot separately, so we see how each player absorbs force, produces it and turns it into jump height, along with any big left-to-right difference.

**Acceleration.** Timed sprints over a short distance. Most sports are won in the first few steps, not at top speed.

**Agility.** Timed change-of-direction drills that show who can stop, turn and go again.

**Strength.** Simple strength tests suited to the age group.

Between rotations, athletes recover in our [recovery lounge](/recovery-lounge/), so the numbers reflect what they can actually do rather than how tired they are.

## What the athletes get

Every player gets their own report with their jump, speed, agility and strength results, plus left and right balance. It's written so a player and a parent can read it, not just a coach.

For a young athlete, seeing their own numbers is often the first time training feels like it's going somewhere. For an older athlete, it's a clear list of what to work on before next season.

## What the coach gets

After the event you get a team recap. It isn't a live leaderboard on the day. It's a short report you can actually use:

- **Rankings by test**, so you can see who's quickest off the mark and who's most explosive
- **Team averages**, compared with a D1 and pro benchmark
- **Players to watch**, including big left-to-right differences that can point to injury risk (more on that in our [return to sport guide](/return-to-sport-after-injury/))
- **Team strengths and team priorities**, so you know what to build into practice

The recap gives your coaching staff something concrete to plan around, and it gives you a baseline to retest against.

## When to run a combine

**Preseason.** The most common choice. You get a baseline for every player before the season starts and a fair look at new players.

**Midseason.** A quick retest shows who's improving and who's falling behind as the season gets heavier.

**Off-season.** Test at the start of the off-season, build the program from the results, and retest before tryouts.

## Who it's for

We've run combines and testing days for club and rep teams, school and youth teams, and senior squads, including youth players at a Raptors 905 clinic and a preseason combine for the MSBA Twins. You can see more of that work on our [community page](/community/).

Testing is adjusted for age, so younger athletes are compared with sensible expectations, not with pros.

## At your facility or ours

We can host your team at Revibe, or bring the force plates and testing gear to your gym or field. Each session takes 10 to 20 athletes. A bigger roster can be split across two sessions.

## After the combine

Some teams use the results on their own. Others want help acting on them. Revibe also offers [team training](/performance-training/team-training/), where our coaches build sessions around your team's priorities, and our clinicians are there when a player needs [physiotherapy](/services/physiotherapy/) or [massage](/services/massage/). Players who want to keep going on their own can book [performance training](/performance-training/).
