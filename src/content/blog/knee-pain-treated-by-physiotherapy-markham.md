## **Common Reasons Knee Pain Occurs**

The knee is one of the largest and hardest-working joints in the body, supporting movement during walking, running, climbing stairs, and many daily activities. Because it absorbs significant force, it is more susceptible to injuries and gradual wear over time. Knee pain may develop suddenly after a fall or sports injury, or it can appear gradually from repetitive strain and overuse. 

Common causes include ligament sprains, meniscus tears, tendon irritation, muscle weakness, and age-related joint changes. Poor movement patterns and improper body mechanics may also increase stress on the knee. Identifying the underlying cause allows a physiotherapist to develop a personalized treatment plan that focuses on restoring function, reducing discomfort, and improving long-term mobility.

## **Sports and Physical Activities That Can Trigger Knee Pain**

Sports and recreational activities often place repeated stress on the knees, increasing the risk of injuries and overuse conditions. Activities such as running, basketball, soccer, tennis, volleyball, and skiing involve jumping, pivoting, quick direction changes, and sudden stops that can strain the knee joint. Even strength training or home workouts performed with poor form may contribute to discomfort over time. 

People who increase their exercise intensity too quickly without proper recovery are also more likely to develop knee pain. Through Physiotherapy Markham, a physiotherapist can assess movement patterns, muscle strength, flexibility, and joint mobility to identify contributing factors and create a personalized rehabilitation plan that supports safe movement and recovery.

## **Medical Conditions Linked to Knee Pain**

Knee pain is not always the result of an injury, as several medical conditions can affect the joint and limit everyday movement. Osteoarthritis is a common cause, particularly in older adults, occurring when cartilage gradually wears down and reduces cushioning between the bones. Rheumatoid arthritis can also cause knee pain by creating inflammation, swelling, and stiffness within the joint. 

Other conditions include patellar tendonitis, bursitis, iliotibial band syndrome, patellofemoral pain syndrome, and degenerative meniscus changes. These conditions can make walking, standing, and climbing stairs uncomfortable. Physiotherapy helps improve joint mobility, strengthen supporting muscles, and increase flexibility, allowing individuals to move more comfortably while supporting better knee function over time.

## **Signs It Is Time to Seek Professional Care**

While occasional soreness after physical activity is common, persistent or worsening knee pain should be evaluated by a healthcare professional. Symptoms such as ongoing pain, swelling that does not improve, difficulty walking, limited range of motion, or a knee that feels unstable may indicate an underlying condition requiring treatment. Some people also experience locking, catching, or discomfort that repeatedly returns during everyday activities. 

Seeking care early may help prevent minor issues from becoming more serious. During an assessment, a physiotherapist evaluates posture, movement patterns, strength, flexibility, balance, and joint mobility to determine the source of the problem. ## **Lifestyle Habits That Support Knee Health**

Healthy lifestyle habits can make a significant difference in maintaining strong, flexible knees and reducing the risk of future discomfort. Maintaining a healthy weight helps decrease pressure on the knee joints during everyday activities such as walking, climbing stairs, and exercising. Regular physical activity strengthens the muscles surrounding the knees, providing better stability and support. 

Stretching before and after exercise can improve flexibility, while wearing supportive footwear helps reduce unnecessary joint stress. It is also beneficial to increase workout intensity gradually and allow adequate recovery between activities to prevent overuse injuries. Following the recommendations provided through Physiotherapy Markham can help individuals develop healthier movement habits that support recovery, improve mobility, and promote long-term joint health.
